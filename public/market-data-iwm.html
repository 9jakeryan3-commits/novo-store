Reading IWM gamma, in plain terms

How to read IWM 0DTE gamma today

IWM's gamma exposure follows the same rules — positive net GEX pins, negative net GEX amplifies — but small-cap liquidity is thinner, so levels matter more, not less. When IWM pushes through its call wall or breaks its put wall, the move can extend further than SPY's because there's less depth to absorb it. The gamma flip marks the regime line, and the Russell's fear gauge (RVX) often tells a different story than the VIX.

Why IWM's own map matters

Small caps lead risk sentiment at turns — RVX can spike while VIX shrugs, and IWM's expected move can be wide relative to its price. Borrowing an S&P read for a Russell scalp misses all of that. FAQ

IWM dealer map — common questions

What is IWM gamma exposure (GEX)?
IWM gamma exposure measures how much dealers must hedge as IWM moves. Positive net GEX damps and pins; negative net GEX amplifies. Small-cap liquidity is thinner, so IWM can run its levels harder — Analyst shows IWM's live net GEX and gamma-flip level.
What is IWM's expected move today?
IWM's expected move is the ±1-standard-deviation range priced by at-the-money implied volatility. Because the Russell's vol (RVX) can diverge from VIX, IWM's expected move often tells its own story. NoVo draws today's IWM expected-move band on the chart live; the exact range is in Analyst.
How is IWM different from SPY for 0DTE?
IWM is small-cap, thinner and gappier, with its own fear gauge (RVX) that can run hot while VIX stays calm. Its dealer walls and expected move sit on different levels than SPY's — NoVo tracks the Russell's own dealer map live alongside SPY and QQQ.
