What he is actually reading

Three tickers, all the way down, and the whole crypto book.

Screeners go wide. NoVo goes deep on the three index ETFs that carry the 0DTE volume, because the difference between a level and a level worth trading is the structure underneath it. Then he reads the crypto map the same way, and the on-chain surface underneath that — one analyst, one archive, whichever question you bring him.

The gamma flip

The line where dealer hedging stops damping moves and starts amplifying them. The most important number on the map — and he gives you the distance to it in percent, not just dollars.

Call wall, put wall, gravity

Where open interest is stacked, which prices dealers are hedged into defending, and the strike that quietly pins the tape while price sits on it.

Net GEX and the profile

Aggregate dealer gamma and its strike-by-strike shape, sampled through the session — so “gamma drained since the open” is a measurement, not a feeling.

Expected move

The range the options market is actually pricing for the day, and how often the close has landed inside it across the sessions he has logged.

Skew, vanna and charm

The second-order flows that move the map without price moving — why levels drift into the close, and why a quiet tape is not the same thing as a stable one.

The crypto dealer map

Gamma by strike on every coin with a real options book, plus the US-listed ETF book beside it, so you see both dealer sets on the same underlying. Funding per venue, never blended into an average that describes nowhere.

Leverage and forced flow

Open interest per venue, 24-hour liquidation flow by side, and the price/OI regime that says whether a move is new money or someone being carried out. He will tell you which venue is paying to be long.

The on-chain surface

The memecoin book on Solana, Base and Robinhood Chain, where there is no options book to read at all, so the structure is liquidity: pooled depth, turnover against it, and which side the wallets are on. Every token keyed to its contract address, because a ticker is a label anyone can mint.

Volatility, per ticker

VIX for SPY, VXN for QQQ, RVX for IWM, each with its percentile against history back to 1990, because “VIX is 15” is a readout, not a read.

Comparison

Two questions nobody else can answer for you.

“How are SPY and QQQ set up against each other right now?”

He reads all three tickers off the same map at the same moment, so the comparison is like for like. Two tickers at the same net GEX are not in the same position if one sits above its flip and the other below it, and that is what he leads with, not the raw number.

“Has today happened before?”

He compares today's structure against every session he has logged, and tells you how many sessions the answer rests on. A few dozen is a count, not “usually”, and he will tell you which one it is.

The rules he works under

Straight about what he is.

Never a signal. No buy, sell or hold, no strikes, no sizing. He reads the map; the trade is yours.
Never a point prediction. He will give you the range the options market is pricing and what setups like this have resolved to, with the sample size. What he will not give you is a closing price he would only be guessing at.
Never your positions. Market structure only. He does not see your account, and does not discuss anyone's P&L.
Never an invented number. Every figure comes from the live map or a lookup he actually ran. If it is in neither, he says he does not have it.
