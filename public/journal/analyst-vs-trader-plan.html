Analyst vs Trader: Which NoVo Plan Actually Fits You

Two plans, one clean dividing line: how fast you need the map. Here's how to tell which side of that line you're on.

NoVo Options Trading · Jul 18, 2026 · live dealer positioning map

NoVo comes in two tiers, and choosing well means being honest about how you actually trade. They are the desk and the cockpit. Analyst ($129/mo, $1,290/yr) is the research desk — where you think; Trader ($209/mo, $2,000/yr) includes Analyst and is the instrument — where you act. The dividing line is not speed for its own sake: it is which seat you trade from.

What Analyst gives you

Analyst is the full structural read: the live dealer-level map (gamma flip, call/put walls, gravity, expected move) on SPY, QQQ and IWM, recomputed every 60 seconds, a written desk note every session, and NoVo — the AI market analyst — to question about any of it. You place your own trades, wherever you already trade. The desk also holds what the cockpit deliberately leaves out: the gamma-by-strike heatmap and its session history, the in-house sweeps-and-blocks flow tape, the closest historical analogues, and push alerts to your phone. Analyst fits the trader who wants NoVo's eyes on the structure and takes it from there.

What Trader adds

Trader adds speed. The same map is drawn on a live candle chart and redrawn about every five seconds, with price moving up to ten times a second, so the levels shift in front of you as the session runs. It also adds an hourly structural audit and NoVo's own scored record of what the setup you are looking at has resolved to before. Analyst is included.

Analyst is the map; Trader is the map at tape speed. Pick by asking how close to the action you actually trade.

How to choose honestly

If you trade off structure and check in a handful of times a session — you want to know where the walls are before you place an order — Analyst is enough. If you scalp intraday and need the levels moving with the candle in front of you, plus the hourly audit and the scored record, Trader is the step up (see the cost-benefit breakdown). Many start on Analyst, then move to Trader when they want it live. Both are month-to-month, so you can change as your needs do.
