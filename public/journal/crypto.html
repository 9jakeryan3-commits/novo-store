Crypto Volatility 12

  • Crypto Skew: What It Prices, and Why the Equity Prior FailsIn equity index options puts are persistently richer than calls. In crypto that is no baseline, so importing the same reading is a permanent false signal.
  • The ETH/BTC Vol Ratio: One Number for the Risk CycleBeta appetite, priced by the two deepest books — and the closest thing to an altseason gauge.
  • DVOL: The Crypto VIX, and How to Read ItDVOL is Deribit's 30-day implied volatility index for BTC and ETH, built the way VIX is. Divide it by 20 and you have the market's implied daily move.
  • Implied vs Realised: What the Options Charged and What HappenedImplied volatility is a price for future movement. Realised is the movement that occurred. The gap between them is the options market being paid, or paying.
  • Measuring Realised Volatility Without Fooling YourselfThe window, the sampling frequency and the annualisation factor are all choices, and each one changes the answer. In a 24/7 market one of them is usually wrong.
  • Scheduled Events: The Volatility You Can See ComingSome catalysts have a date on them. The options market prices them in advance, which is why being right about the event is often not enough.
  • Term Structure in a Market That Never ClosesThe curve of implied volatility across expiries prices uncertainty over time. Continuous trading and daily expiries give crypto a shape of its own.
  • The Volatility Surface, and What Its Shape SaysImplied volatility across every strike and expiry at once. The surface is where skew and term structure stop being two charts and become one object.
  • Volatility Cones: Is This Level Actually Unusual?A cone plots realised volatility percentiles across horizons. It answers the question a single volatility number cannot: unusual compared with what?
  • Volatility Regimes, and Why the Same Level Means Two ThingsMarkets persist in states rather than drifting randomly between them. Identifying which state you are in matters more than the level itself.
  • Volatility of Volatility: How Stable Is the Regime?Implied volatility has its own volatility. When that rises, the market is uncertain about its own uncertainty - and options on options get expensive.
  • Why Volatility Clusters, and What Sustains It in CryptoBig moves follow big moves in every market. In crypto the mechanism is largely leverage, which makes the clustering sharper and shorter.

Crypto Costs 10

  • Funding Is Rent, and Being Early Pays ItA correct call held too long through positive funding can lose to a wrong call held briefly. Carry is a real cost with a real clock.
  • Maker and Taker Fees: You Are Being Paid to WaitProviding liquidity costs less than removing it, sometimes less than nothing. The gap is usually larger than the spread you were trying to avoid.
  • One Asset, Many Books: What Fragmentation CostsThe same coin trades on many venues with no consolidated tape. Price, depth and fees all differ, and nothing automatically reconciles them.
  • Order Types Are Not Standardised HereThe same order type can behave differently across crypto venues. Assuming equity semantics produces fills you did not intend.
  • Reading Order Book Depth Without Trusting ItThe book shows resting size at every level. Much of it will not be there when you arrive, and that is not necessarily deception.
  • Reading a Fee Schedule ProperlyHeadline rates are the smallest part. Tiers, settlement type and funding turn a quoted fee into a very different realised cost.
  • Slicing an Order: Trading Time Against ImpactBreaking a large order into pieces reduces price impact and increases exposure to the market moving. There is no setting that avoids both.
  • Spreads Widen Exactly When You Need Them TightMarket makers widen as uncertainty rises, so the cost of trading peaks during the moves that make you want to trade. That is rational, not hostile.
  • The Round Trip Is the Only Cost That MattersFees, spread, impact and funding are quoted separately and paid together. Adding them up is what tells you whether an edge survives.
  • What It Actually Costs to Trade Crypto on RobinhoodRobinhood crypto is commission-free, but not free. The markup is disclosed in its own API, and a round trip typically costs around 1.9%.

  • Overtrading Is the Default in a 24/7 MarketEvery market punishes overtrading. Only this one removes the constraint that used to limit how much of it was possible.
  • Ten Positions, One BetHolding many coins feels diversified and usually is not. Most of the crypto market moves together, especially when it matters.

  • Crypto as a Risk-Appetite GaugeAn asset with no cash flows and continuous trading reacts to risk appetite faster than most markets - which makes it informative about them.
