Crypto Market Structure

Dealer gamma, perpetual funding, forced liquidation flow and on-chain liquidity.

NoVo Options Trading · Aug 28, 2026 · the live crypto map

Dealer gamma, perpetual funding, forced liquidation flow and on-chain liquidity.

47 articles in this section of the Journal, grouped by topic. Every one is free to read.

On-Chain Structure 20

  • A Ticker Is Not a TokenOn-chain a symbol is a string anyone can write, and one ticker can cover many tokens. The contract address is the only thing identifying what you actually buy.
  • Gas Fees Are a Trading Cost, Not a TechnicalityA fixed cost per transaction changes which strategies are viable and which position sizes make sense. It has no real equity analogue.
  • Holder Concentration: Many Wallets or a FewThe same market cap can be held by thousands of wallets or by five. It is the same number describing two completely different exit risks.
  • How an AMM Prices a TokenThere is no order book and no quote. An automated market maker derives price from the ratio of what is in the pool, which changes how every trade behaves.
  • Impermanent Loss, and Why Liquidity LeavesA pool automatically sells what is rising and buys what is falling. That is the whole mechanism, and it explains why depth departs from tokens doing nothing wrong.
  • Liquidity Added or Pulled: Depth as a FlowA pool depth reading is a stock. The useful signal is the direction it is moving - and that only exists if something was recording yesterday.
  • MEV and Sandwich Attacks: The Cost You Cannot See on the ChartYour order is visible before it settles, and that visibility is worth money to someone else. What MEV extracts, and which part of it you can actually defend against.
  • On-Chain Liquidity vs an Order BookMost tokens have no options book, so there is no gamma to read. What they have is liquidity structure - and when the real risk is the exit, that is the better read.
  • Pool Depth Is Not VolumeTwo numbers constantly reported side by side and constantly conflated. One says what you can trade; the other says what was traded. They can point opposite ways.
  • Slippage and Price Impact Are Not the Same ThingOne is the cost of your own size against the curve; the other is what changed between quote and confirmation. Traders conflate them and mis-price both.
  • Solana vs EVM: Two Different MicrostructuresThe same trade behaves differently depending on the chain underneath it. Block times, fee models and pool designs differ enough to change execution outcomes.
  • The Liquidity Lifecycle of a New TokenDepth on a new token follows a recognisable arc rather than a random walk. Knowing which stage you are in changes what the same depth reading means.
  • Tokenised Stocks and a Tape That ClosesA stock token trades continuously while the equity it references does not. That gap is structural, and it is not the arbitrage it looks like.
  • What Is Robinhood Chain, and Why Does It Matter to a Trader?A broker running its own settlement layer changes where liquidity forms. What Robinhood Chain is, and what it means structurally rather than as an announcement.
  • What the Quote Asset Tells YouA token paired against a stablecoin, against the gas asset, or against another token are three different instruments with three different risks.
  • When the Exit Does Not Exist: Honeypots and Unsellable TokensSome tokens can be bought and not sold, by design in the contract itself. The chart looks perfect and the sell never executes.
  • Where Is the Price Actually Made?Pools and exchanges quote the same asset and one of them is usually following. Knowing which changes what a divergence means.
  • Why Wrapped Assets Exist, and What They Cost YouWETH, WBTC and their relatives are plumbing, not products. Knowing why they exist explains several things that otherwise look like duplicate markets.
  • Why a Thin Pool Behaves Like a Short-Gamma RegimeShallow depth and negative dealer gamma are different mechanisms that produce the same behaviour: moves that feed themselves.
  • Why the Biggest Pool on a Chain Is Usually PlumbingRank any chain by turnover and the gas asset against its stablecoin wins every time. That pool is infrastructure, and counting it as demand corrupts the ranking.

Perps & Funding 10

  • Funding Arbitrage Between VenuesWhen two venues disagree on funding, the spread is capturable. What it costs to capture is why the disagreement persists.
  • Funding Extremes: What Actually FollowsAn extreme funding print is a real event with a real base rate. Reading it needs a reference window and an honest sample, not an eyeball.
  • Funding Is a Crowding Gauge, Not a Direction SignalPositive funding says longs are paying to stay long. It does not say they are wrong, and trading it as a fade is how the number gets misused.
  • Funding Schedules Differ, and It Changes the CostEight hours, four, or one. The interval a venue uses changes what the same headline rate costs you, and makes cross-venue comparison misleading.
  • Open Interest Is How Much Leverage Is in the SystemOpen interest counts contracts outstanding, not activity. Rising OI means positions are being built; falling OI means they are being closed.
  • The Cash-and-Carry Trade, and What It Really CostsBuy spot, short the perpetual, collect funding. The delta is neutral and the risks that remain are the ones that end the trade.
  • The Long/Short Ratio, and Why It Rarely Means What People ThinkThe number counts accounts or positions on one venue, not exposure. A ratio can lean hard one way while the money leans the other.
  • What Is a Perpetual Swap?A futures contract with no expiry should drift away from spot forever. Funding is the mechanism that stops it, and it is what makes the instrument readable.
  • Why Funding Should Never Be Blended Across VenuesAveraging funding across exchanges destroys the only thing it was telling you. The disagreement between venues is the signal, not noise to be smoothed away.
  • Why Perpetuals Took Over From Dated FuturesCrypto trades a contract that barely exists in traditional markets. The reasons are structural, and they explain the data you get.

Margin & Liquidation 8

  • Coin-Margined vs Stablecoin-Margined: The Reflexivity ProblemPost the same asset you are trading as collateral and your margin falls exactly when your position does. That feedback is why cascades run further.
  • Initial and Maintenance Margin, and the Gap Between ThemOne number decides what you can open, a smaller one decides when you are closed. The distance between them is the room your trade actually has.
  • Insurance Funds and Auto-DeleveragingWhen a liquidation closes worse than bankruptcy price, someone absorbs the shortfall. If the insurance fund cannot, the winning traders do.
  • Isolated vs Cross Margin: Which Account Is at RiskIsolated margin caps the loss at one position. Cross margin puts the whole balance behind every position. Neither is safer in general - they fail differently.
  • Liquidation Heatmaps: What They Show and What They Do NotA liquidation map is an estimate of where forced flow would appear, built from assumptions about leverage. Useful as structure, misleading as a target.
  • Mark Price vs Last Price: Which One Liquidates YouYour position is not valued at the last trade. It is valued at the mark, and the gap between the two is why a wick can miss you or take you out.
  • Socialised Loss: When the Venue Is the RiskEvery derivatives position has a counterparty, and in crypto it is the exchange and, ultimately, the other traders on it. That risk has no price on your screen.
  • Where Your Liquidation Price Actually SitsLeverage is usually quoted as a multiple. The number that matters is the percentage move against you that ends the position, and it is not linear.

Crypto Positioning 4

  • Crypto Funding Rates: A Published Price for CrowdingFunding is the periodic payment between longs and shorts that holds a perpetual future near spot. It is the clearest read on which side of a crypto trade is crowded - and equities have no equivalent.
  • Liquidation Cascades: Forced Flow at Prices You Can ComputeA liquidation cascade is forced selling that has to happen, at levels derived from open interest and leverage. It is the crypto answer to a gamma squeeze.
  • Open Interest and Price: The Four RegimesPrice and open interest moving together or apart sorts crypto into four regimes - new longs, new shorts, short covering and long liquidation. It is the cleanest positioning read in the market.
  • Spot-Perp Basis: Reading the Price of LeverageThe gap between a perpetual future and spot is the market's price for leveraged exposure. Widening basis is leverage arriving; a collapsing one is leverage leaving.

Crypto Dealer Flow 3

  • Bitcoin Gamma Exposure (GEX), ExplainedBitcoin has a dealer map. Deribit carries almost all crypto options open interest, so BTC gamma exposure can be computed the same way it is for SPY - and it moves spot the same way.
  • IBIT Is Not Bitcoin: Two Books, Two MapsBTC and ETH each sit under two options books - crypto-native and US-listed ETF. Different multipliers, hours and expiries; reading one as the other is an error.
  • The 08:00 UTC Expiry: Why Crypto Pins Every Single DayDeribit options expire daily at 08:00 UTC. That gives crypto a pinning study with 365 samples a year, where equity monthly opex offers twelve.

Crypto Costs 1

  • What It Actually Costs to Trade Crypto on RobinhoodRobinhood crypto is commission-free, but not free. The markup is disclosed in its own API, and a round trip typically costs around 1.9%.

Crypto Volatility 1

  • DVOL: The Crypto VIX, and How to Read ItDVOL is Deribit's 30-day implied volatility index for BTC and ETH, built the way VIX is. Divide it by 20 and you have the market's implied daily move.
